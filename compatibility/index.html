CoolTouch for Mac / Compatibility

A GOOD FIT STARTS HERE

Your Mac. A compatible touchscreen.

macOS does not provide native touch and gesture support for every external USB touchscreen. CoolTouch fills that gap on compatible hardware. It doesn’t add touch to a MacBook’s built-in screen.

What CoolTouch requires

CoolTouch for Mac 2.2.1 requires an Apple silicon Mac (M1 or later), macOS 15 or later, and a compatible USB touch controller. Both video and USB touch input must reach the Mac. Some USB-C cables carry both.

Your screen must send supported touch input over USB. A video cable alone isn’t enough.

How CoolTouch checks compatibility

Compatibility depends on the touchscreen controller and its input format. CoolTouch checks the touch input and can guide you through supported alternatives, but we do not promise universal compatibility. Not every controller or input format is supported.

First-time setup guides you to verify single-finger and two-finger input. When needed, it asks you to choose the corresponding display. Where other controller modes are available, CoolTouch may offer an alternative mode.

If your screen is unverified

We do not publish a brand or chip list, and we do not claim that CoolTouch works with every USB touchscreen. If your screen has not been verified, use the 7-day full-feature trial on your own Mac. No card is required, and the trial ends without an automatic charge.

Unsure about your setup? Ask us at support@cooltouch.app before buying.

macOS 14 and earlier CoolTouch versions

Using macOS 14? Keep your compatible version. The 2.2.1 update requires macOS 15 or later. If you already purchased CoolTouch, use the download and license key in your original Gumroad library.

What CoolTouch does not cover

CoolTouch does not add touch to a MacBook’s built-in screen. A compatible external touchscreen is separate hardware.

Gesture behavior depends on the app, the region you touch, and your macOS settings. Supported examples include pinch-zoom in Chrome (including independent zoom on supported embedded maps), PDF scrolling and pinch-zoom in Preview, step zoom in Photos, two-finger rotation in Apple Maps, vertical and horizontal scrolling in Music, and tap, double-tap, long-press menus and dragging in Finder. Apple Maps two-finger rotation is distinct from Chrome pinch-zoom on supported embedded maps. Photos zoom is step-based, not a native continuous zoom.
